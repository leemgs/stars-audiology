\begin{abstract}
\noindent\textbf{Purpose:} STARS tests, in nationally representative survey data, whether \emph{perceived stress} is associated with the tinnitus \emph{symptom} and with the audiometric \emph{threshold}, and whether these associations dissociate. Analyses, variables, cycles, and adjustment sets were prespecified to limit analyst degrees of freedom. Because the surveys carry no validated occupational-stress instrument, the exposure is labeled perceived stress (a single general item), not occupational stress, and every estimate is an association, not a cause.

\textbf{Method:} KNHANES V (2010--2012; adults 40--69) is the primary cohort because it carries a general perceived-stress item; U.S. NHANES (2011--2018) provides supporting analyses under a PHQ-9 distress proxy. A directed acyclic graph fixes the minimal sufficient adjustment set (age, sex, occupational noise) and separates confounders from mediators. Tinnitus and better-/worse-ear pure-tone average are modeled with survey-weighted (Taylor-linearized) regression, per STROBE.

\textbf{Results (KNHANES):} High perceived stress was associated with \emph{tinnitus} (OR 1.42, 1.26--1.60) but only weakly and non-significantly with the \emph{audiometric threshold} (OR 1.18, 0.99--1.41), which was dominated by noise and age---the prespecified dissociation of hypothesis H1. The tinnitus association survived adjustment for depressed mood and audiometric hearing loss and persisted for non-bothersome tinnitus. NHANES showed the same directional pattern (directional consistency, not validation). A cross-national validation plan and a clinician-governed AI/referral-safety component are prospective (no results).

\textbf{Conclusions:} In representative Korean adults, perceived stress tracks the tinnitus symptom but not the audiometric threshold once noise and age are accounted for---consistent with a partly central, non-cochlear correlate of tinnitus. Findings are reproducible from committed code; because the design is cross-sectional, directionality (including reverse causation) is unresolved, and prospective cohorts remain necessary.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} tinnitus; bothersome tinnitus; hearing loss; audiometry; perceived stress; work-related factors; KNHANES; NHANES; survey-weighted analysis; reproducibility; audiology.
