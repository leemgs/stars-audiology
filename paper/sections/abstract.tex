\begin{abstract}
\noindent\textbf{Purpose:} STARS (Stress, Tinnitus, and Audiometric Research Study) tests, in nationally representative survey data, whether \emph{perceived stress} is associated with the tinnitus \emph{symptom} and with the audiometric \emph{threshold}, and whether these two associations dissociate. Analyses, variable definitions, survey cycles, and adjustment sets were prespecified to limit analyst degrees of freedom. Because the surveys carry no validated occupational-stress instrument, the exposure is labeled perceived stress (a single general item), not measured occupational stress, and every estimate is an association, not a causal effect.

\textbf{Method:} The Korea National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey (KNHANES V, 2010--2012; adults 40--69) is the primary cohort (it carries a general perceived-stress item); U.S. NHANES (2011--2018) provides supporting analyses under a PHQ-9 distress proxy. A causal directed acyclic graph defines the minimal sufficient adjustment set (age, sex, occupational noise) and separates confounders from mediators. Tinnitus and better-/worse-ear pure-tone average are modeled with survey-weighted (Taylor-linearized) regression, reported per STROBE.

\textbf{Primary results (KNHANES):} Survey-weighted tinnitus prevalence was 21.1\% (95\% CI 19.9--22.3) and better-ear hearing loss ($>$25 dB HL) 15.4\%. High perceived stress was associated with \emph{tinnitus} (OR 1.42, 1.26--1.60, $p<0.001$) but only weakly and non-significantly with the \emph{audiometric threshold} (OR 1.18, 0.99--1.41, $p=0.07$), which was instead dominated by occupational noise and age. This symptom-versus-threshold dissociation is the prespecified pattern of hypothesis H1. Under a PHQ-9 distress proxy, NHANES showed the same directional pattern---directional consistency, not external validation of the stress exposure. A cross-national validation plan and a clinician-governed AI/SSNHL referral-safety component are prespecified but prospective (no results).

\textbf{Conclusions:} In representative Korean adults, perceived stress tracks the tinnitus symptom but not the audiometric threshold once noise and age are accounted for---consistent with a partly central, non-cochlear correlate of tinnitus. The result is reproducible from committed code; because the design is cross-sectional, directionality (including reverse causation) is unresolved, and prospective cohorts remain necessary for timing, recovery, and prognosis.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} tinnitus; bothersome tinnitus; hearing loss; audiometry; perceived stress; work-related factors; KNHANES; NHANES; survey-weighted analysis; reproducibility; audiology.
