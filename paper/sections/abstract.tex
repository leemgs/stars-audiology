\begin{abstract}
\noindent\textbf{Purpose:} STARS tests, in nationally representative survey data, whether \emph{perceived stress} dissociates the tinnitus \emph{symptom} from the audiometric \emph{threshold}. Analyses, variables, cycles, and adjustment sets were prespecified. The exposure is perceived stress (a single general item), not occupational stress, and every estimate is an association, not a cause.

\textbf{Method:} KNHANES V (2010--2012; adults 40--69) is the primary cohort because it carries a general perceived-stress item; U.S. NHANES (2011--2018) provides supporting analyses under a PHQ-9 distress proxy. A directed acyclic graph fixes the minimal-sufficient adjustment set (age, sex, occupational noise) and separates confounders from mediators. Outcomes are modeled with survey-weighted (Taylor-linearized) regression, per STROBE.

\textbf{Results (KNHANES):} High perceived stress was associated with \emph{tinnitus} (OR 1.42, 95\% CI 1.26--1.60) but more weakly with \emph{better-ear hearing loss} (OR 1.18, 0.99--1.41), the prespecified contrast (H1). The tinnitus association persisted after adjustment for depressed mood and audiometric hearing loss and for non-bothersome tinnitus. After excluding middle-ear pathology by bilateral tympanometry, the hearing-loss association strengthened (OR 1.29, 1.07--1.55); the contrast is therefore one of degree, not presence versus absence. NHANES showed directional consistency under a distinct proxy, not external validation.

\textbf{Conclusions:} In representative Korean adults, perceived stress tracked the tinnitus symptom more strongly than better-ear hearing loss after adjustment. The cross-sectional, single-item measures preclude causal or mechanistic inference; reverse causation remains plausible.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} tinnitus; hearing loss; audiometry; perceived stress; KNHANES; survey-weighted analysis.
